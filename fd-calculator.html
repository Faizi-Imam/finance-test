Understanding Fixed Deposits (FD)

A Fixed Deposit (FD) is a financial instrument offered by banks and Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) in India. In an FD, you invest a sum of money for a fixed tenure at a predetermined rate of interest. The interest rate remains locked throughout the tenure, making FDs one of the safest debt investment options available.

The Compound Interest Formula for FDs

Most commercial banks in India calculate FD interest on a quarterly compounding basis. The formula is:

A = P × [1 + (r / n)]^(n × t)

Where:

  • A is the final Maturity Amount.
  • P is the Principal deposit amount.
  • r is the annual interest rate (expressed as a decimal, e.g., 7% is 0.07).
  • n is the compounding frequency per year (e.g., n = 4 for quarterly, n = 12 for monthly, n = 1 for yearly).
  • t is the tenure of the deposit in years.

Step-by-Step Example Calculation

Let's calculate the maturity value for a deposit of ₹1,00,000 (P) at an interest rate of 7.0% for 3 years (t) with standard **quarterly compounding (n = 4)**.

  1. Convert interest rate to decimal (r): 7 / 100 = 0.07.
  2. Compounding periods (n): 4 (four quarters per year).
  3. Plug variables into the equation:
    A = 100,000 × [1 + (0.07 / 4)]^(4 × 3)
    A = 100,000 × [1 + 0.0175]^12
    A = 100,000 × (1.0175)^12
  4. Evaluate compound interest factor:
    (1.0175)^12 ≈ 1.231439
  5. Multiply by principal:
    Maturity Value (A) = 100,000 × 1.231439 ≈ ₹1,23,144.
    Total Interest Earned = ₹1,23,144 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹23,144.

If you compound yearly (n=1) instead of quarterly, the maturity amount would be ₹1,22,504. Compounding quarterly earns you an extra ₹640 due to interest compounding on top of interest more frequently.

Why Banks Compund FDs Quarterly in India

By default, Indian scheduled commercial banks compound fixed deposit interest every quarter (on 30th June, 30th September, 31st December, and 31st March). While you can opt for monthly interest payouts (which slightly reduces the final payout due to discounting) or yearly payouts, the cumulative option with quarterly compounding maximizes your returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is FD interest taxable in India?

Yes. The interest earned on fixed deposits is fully taxable under the head "Income from Other Sources" at your applicable income tax slab rates. If the interest income across all FDs in a bank exceeds ₹40,000 in a financial year (₹50,000 for senior citizens), the bank will deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) at 10%.

Can I withdraw my Fixed Deposit before maturity?

Yes, premature withdrawal is allowed for most FDs. However, banks charge a penalty (typically 0.5% to 1.0% reduction in the applicable interest rate for the duration the deposit was held). Some special deposits are locked-in and do not allow premature payouts.

What is a Tax-Saving Fixed Deposit?

A Tax-Saving FD is a special term deposit that qualifies for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act (up to ₹1.5 Lakhs per year). These deposits have a mandatory **5-year lock-in period**, during which you cannot make premature withdrawals or take loans against the deposit.
