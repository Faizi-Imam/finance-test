Understanding Goods and Services Tax (GST)

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is an indirect tax levied on the supply of goods and services in India. Introduced on 1st July 2017, it replaced multiple indirect taxes such as VAT, service tax, excise duty, and octroi, establishing a unified "One Nation, One Tax" system.

CGST, SGST, and IGST Split Explained

Under India's dual GST structure, taxes are split depending on where the transaction occurs:

  • CGST (Central GST): Collected by the Central Government on intra-state supplies of goods or services.
  • SGST (State GST): Collected by the State Government on intra-state supplies.
    Note: For intra-state sales, the GST slab rate is split equally (50:50) into CGST and SGST. For example, an 18% GST rate consists of 9% CGST and 9% SGST.
  • IGST (Integrated GST): Levied by the Central Government on inter-state supplies (sales from one state to another) and imports. The IGST rate is equal to the total GST slab rate.

GST Mathematical Formulas

1. GST Exclusive (Adding GST to Base Price)

Use this if you know the net base price and want to calculate the tax and total cost:

GST Amount = Base Price × [Rate / 100]
Total Price = Base Price + GST Amount

Example: Net Price is ₹10,000 and GST rate is 18%.
GST Amount = 10,000 × 0.18 = ₹1,800
Total Price = 10,000 + 1,800 = ₹11,800
CGST = ₹900, SGST = ₹900.

2. GST Inclusive (Extracting GST from Total Price)

Use this if you know the final bill amount and want to extract the tax component and base cost:

Base Price = Total Price × [100 / (100 + Rate)]
GST Amount = Total Price - Base Price

Example: Bill Total is ₹11,800 and GST rate is 18%.
Base Price = 11,800 × [100 / 118] = ₹10,000
GST Amount = 11,800 - 10,000 = ₹1,800
CGST = ₹900, SGST = ₹900.

GST Tax Slabs in India

Goods and services are categorized under five primary tax slabs:

  • 0% (Exempt): Essential goods like milk, fresh fruits, vegetables, and flour.
  • 5%: Essential items like sugar, tea, coffee, edible oil, and life-saving drugs.
  • 12%: Butter, cheese, cell phones, processed food, and business class air travel.
  • 18%: Most services, IT services, financial services, steel, and capital goods.
  • 28%: Luxury and luxury/sin items like cars, soft drinks, tobacco products, and five-star hotel services.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who needs to register for GST in India?

Businesses providing services with an annual turnover exceeding **₹20 Lakhs** (₹10 Lakhs for North-Eastern states) and suppliers of goods with turnover exceeding **₹40 Lakhs** must register for GST. Additionally, anyone conducting inter-state trade or e-commerce sales must register regardless of turnover.

What is Input Tax Credit (ITC)?

Input Tax Credit allows businesses to reduce the GST they collect on sales by the amount of GST they already paid on business purchases (inputs). This prevents double taxation and cascading taxes along the supply chain.

Is there any difference between CGST + SGST vs IGST payouts?

No. Economically, the tax burden is exactly the same. For intra-state trade, a 18% rate is paid as 9% CGST and 9% SGST. For inter-state trade, a 18% rate is paid as 18% IGST. The total tax liability remains identical.
