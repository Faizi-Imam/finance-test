What is Compound Interest?

Albert Einstein famously declared compound interest to be the "eighth wonder of the world". Unlike simple interest, which is calculated strictly on the initial principal sum, compound interest is calculated on the initial principal and the accumulated interest of previous periods. Effectively, you earn "interest on interest", creating exponential growth.

The Compound Interest Formula

The standard compounding formula is written as:

A = P × [1 + (r / n)]^(n × t)

Where:

  • A is the final Accumulated Amount (maturity value).
  • P is the Principal investment.
  • r is the nominal annual interest rate (as a decimal).
  • n is the number of times interest is compounded per year.
  • t is the total time period in years.

Step-by-Step Example Calculation

Suppose you invest ₹1,00,000 (P) at an annual interest rate of 10% (r = 0.10) for a tenure of 5 years (t = 5) compounded **quarterly (n = 4)**.

  1. Compounding period fraction: r / n = 0.10 / 4 = 0.025.
  2. Total compounding cycles: n × t = 4 × 5 = 20 compounding cycles.
  3. Apply the formula:
    A = 100,000 × (1 + 0.025)^20
    A = 100,000 × (1.025)^20
  4. Evaluate the compound factor:
    (1.025)^20 ≈ 1.638616
  5. Find the maturity value:
    A = 100,000 × 1.638616 ≈ ₹1,63,862.
    Total Compound Interest Earned = ₹1,63,862 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹63,862.

Compare this to simple interest: 100,000 × 10% × 5 = ₹50,000. Compounding quarterly earns you an extra **₹13,862** over simple interest on the same ₹1,00,000 principal!

The Rule of 72: Double Your Money

The **Rule of 72** is a quick, handy shortcut to estimate how many years it will take for your money to double at a given annual interest rate under compounding:

Years to Double ≈ 72 / Annual Interest Rate

For example, if you earn a **10%** annual return: 72 / 10 = **7.2 years** to double your capital. If you earn **12%**: 72 / 12 = **6 years** to double your money.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does compounding frequency affect my returns?

The more frequently interest is compounded, the higher your final payout will be. For instance, compounding monthly yields slightly more than compounding quarterly, which in turn yields more than compounding yearly, because interest is added to the principal balance more often.

What is the difference between Nominal Rate and APY?

The Nominal Rate is the stated annual interest rate (e.g., 10%). The Annual Percentage Yield (APY), or Effective Annual Rate, is the actual rate of return taking compounding into account. For 10% compounded quarterly, the APY is **10.38%**.

How can I make compounding work best for me?

Compounding needs two key inputs to perform miracles: **time** and **consistency**. Start investing as early in life as possible, even with small sums, and let the assets accumulate over decades to maximize growth.
