Old vs New Tax Regime – Slabs for FY 2025-26

In India, taxpayers can choose between two tax systems. The **Old Tax Regime** permits standard deductions (Section 80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest, etc.) but has higher slab rates. The **New Tax Regime** offers lower slab rates and a higher rebate limit, but requires you to forfeit almost all deductions.

Standard Deductions and Rebate Section 87A

  • Standard Deduction: Salaried employees get a standard deduction of **₹75,000** under the New Regime and **₹50,000** under the Old Regime for FY 2025-26.
  • Rebate Limit: Under Section 87A, you pay zero tax if your taxable income does not exceed **₹12,00,000** in the New Regime. In the Old Regime, the rebate threshold is set much lower at **₹5,00,000**.
  • Health and Education Cess: A flat **4% cess** is added to the calculated tax liability under both regimes.

Step-by-Step Example Comparison

Suppose your Gross Salary is ₹12,00,000 and you have ₹2,00,000 in exemptions (80C investments, health insurance, HRA, etc.).

1. New Tax Regime Calculation:

  1. Apply Standard Deduction: ₹12,00,000 - ₹75,000 = ₹11,25,000 (Taxable Income).
  2. Since taxable income is below the rebate threshold of ₹12,00,000, you qualify for full rebate under Section 87A.
  3. Total Tax Payable = ₹0.

2. Old Tax Regime Calculation:

  1. Apply Standard Deduction and Exemptions: ₹12,00,000 - ₹50,000 - ₹2,00,000 = ₹9,50,000 (Taxable Income).
  2. Apply Old Regime tax slabs:
    - Up to ₹2.5L: Nil
    - ₹2.5L to ₹5L (5% of 2.5L): ₹12,500
    - ₹5L to ₹9.5L (20% of 4.5L): ₹90,000
    - Base Tax = ₹12,500 + ₹90,000 = ₹1,02,500.
  3. Add Cess (4%): 1,02,500 × 0.04 = ₹4,100.
  4. Total Tax Payable = ₹1,06,600.

In this case, the **New Regime saves you ₹1,06,600** in tax payments. In general, unless your tax-deductible investments exceed ₹3.75 Lakhs, the New Regime is more beneficial for most taxpayers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I switch tax regimes every year?

Salaried taxpayers with no business income can choose their preferred tax regime every financial year at the time of filing their Income Tax Return (ITR). However, individuals with business or professional income can only switch regimes once in a lifetime.

What is the default tax regime in India?

From FY 2023-24 onwards, the **New Tax Regime is the default tax regime**. If you want to file your taxes using the Old Regime, you must actively opt-in by submitting Form 10-IEA before filing your ITR.

Is HRA deduction allowed under the New Tax Regime?

No. House Rent Allowance (HRA), Section 80C (PPF, LIC, EPF, ELSS, School Fees), Section 80D (Health Insurance premium), and home loan interest on self-occupied property (Section 24b) are **not allowed** as deductions under the New Regime.
