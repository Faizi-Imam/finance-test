What is a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is an investment vehicle offered by mutual funds, allowing investors to invest small amounts periodically (daily, monthly, or quarterly) instead of making heavy one-time lumpsum payments. It promotes financial discipline and leverages the power of compounding and rupee cost averaging.

The SIP Compound Interest Formula

SIP future values are calculated using a compound interest annuity formula because investments are made periodically rather than all at once. The formula is:

FV = P × [ ( (1 + r)^n - 1 ) / r ] × (1 + r)

Where:

  • FV is the Future Value of the investment portfolio (maturity amount).
  • P is the Monthly SIP amount.
  • r is the monthly rate of return (annual return divided by 12, then divided by 100).
  • n is the total number of monthly payments (years × 12).

Step-by-Step Example Calculation

Suppose you set up a monthly SIP of ₹5,000 (P) at an expected annual return of 12% for a tenure of 10 years (120 months).

  1. Calculate monthly return rate (r): 12 / 12 / 100 = 0.01.
  2. Calculate number of instalments (n): 10 × 12 = 120 months.
  3. Calculate compound interest factor:
    (1 + r)^n = (1.01)^120 ≈ 3.30039
  4. Apply the SIP annuity formula:
    FV = 5,000 × [ (3.30039 - 1) / 0.01 ] × (1 + 0.01)
    FV = 5,000 × [ 2.30039 / 0.01 ] × 1.01
    FV = 5,000 × 230.039 × 1.01 ≈ ₹11,61,695
  5. Calculate total invested capital vs gained returns:
    Total Invested Sum = 5,000 × 120 = ₹6,00,000.
    Wealth Gained (Returns) = ₹11,61,695 - ₹6,00,000 = ₹5,61,695.

In this example, your total wealth grows to over ₹11.6 Lakhs. Notice that almost 48% of the final portfolio value consists of compound interest gains. If you double the tenure to 20 years, the invested amount becomes ₹12 Lakhs, but the total value skyrockets to over ₹49.9 Lakhs, representing the true "compounding hockey-stick curve".

Key Benefits of SIP Mutual Funds

  • Rupee Cost Averaging: Since you invest a fixed amount regularly, you buy more mutual fund units when market prices are low and fewer units when prices are high. This averages out the cost of acquisition over time, neutralizing market volatility.
  • Compounding Benefits: Earnings from your units are reinvested. Over long horizons, the reinvested returns compound, multiplying your wealth exponentially.
  • Lower Barrier to Entry: Most mutual funds in India allow you to start a SIP with as little as ₹500 or ₹1,000 per month, making it accessible to students, salaried professionals, and beginners.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are SIP returns guaranteed?

No. SIP investments in mutual funds are subject to market risks. Equity mutual funds track stock indices, meaning returns fluctuate. While the calculator uses a fixed rate (e.g., 12% p.a.), actual historical long-term equity SIPs in India have yielded between 10% and 15% CAGR.

What is the difference between SIP and Lumpsum investing?

A SIP involves investing a fixed sum periodically, while a lumpsum is a one-time investment. Lumpsum is preferred when you have a large cash surplus and markets are undervalued. SIP is better for regular savings, shielding you from timing the stock market.

Can I pause or stop my SIP at any time?

Yes. Unlike fixed deposits or insurance policies, SIPs are highly flexible. You can pause or stop your monthly auto-debit payments via your broker or mutual fund house without any exit penalties, and your accumulated units will continue to stay invested.
