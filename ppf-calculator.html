What is the Public Provident Fund (PPF)?

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a popular long-term savings-cum-investment scheme in India. Launched by the Ministry of Finance in 1968, it aims to mobilize small savings by offering reasonable returns combined with high tax safety. Because it is backed by the Central Government, the principal and interest earnings carry an absolute sovereign guarantee.

The PPF Compounding Formula

Interest on PPF is compounded annually. The mathematical formula to compute PPF maturity value is:

A = F × [ ( (1 + r)^n - 1 ) / r ] × (1 + r)

Where:

  • A is the final Maturity Amount.
  • F is the Annual Installment deposited.
  • r is the annual interest rate (e.g., 7.1% p.a. is 0.071).
  • n is the total number of compounding years (minimum 15).

Note: This equation assumes that the annual installment is deposited at the beginning of each financial year (before 5th April).

Step-by-Step Example Calculation

Suppose you deposit the maximum limit of ₹1,50,000 (F) annually at the beginning of the year for the standard 15-year tenure (n) at the rate of 7.1% (r = 0.071).

  1. Calculate the compound interest factor:
    (1 + r)^n = (1.071)^15 ≈ 2.78457
  2. Apply the formula:
    A = 150,000 × [ (2.78457 - 1) / 0.071 ] × (1 + 0.071)
    A = 150,000 × [ 1.78457 / 0.071 ] × 1.071
    A = 150,000 × 25.13479 × 1.071 ≈ ₹40,36,654
  3. Evaluate investments vs interest:
    Total Invested Sum = 1,50,000 × 15 = ₹22,50,000.
    Interest Earned = ₹40,36,654 - ₹22,50,000 = ₹17,86,654.

By contributing ₹1.5 Lakhs annually, you accumulate over ₹40.36 Lakhs by maturity. The earned interest of ₹17.8 Lakhs is completely tax-free!

The EEE Tax Status Explained

PPF enjoys the coveted Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) status under Section 80C of the Indian Income Tax Act:

  • Exempt 1 (Investment): The annual principal deposit (up to ₹1.5 Lakhs) is deductible from your taxable income.
  • Exempt 2 (Accumulation): The compounding interest accumulated annually in your account is completely exempt from wealth tax or income tax.
  • Exempt 3 (Withdrawal): The final maturity amount paid out at the end of the lock-in period is completely tax-free.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the deposit limits for a PPF account?

You must invest a minimum of **₹500** and can invest a maximum of **₹1,50,000** per financial year. If you invest more than ₹1.5 Lakhs, the excess amount will not earn any interest and will not be eligible for tax deductions.

Can I extend my PPF account after 15 years?

Yes. Upon completion of the initial 15-year tenure, you can extend your PPF account indefinitely in blocks of **5 years**. You must submit Form H to your bank or post office within 1 year of maturity if you wish to continue making contributions.

When is PPF interest calculated and credited?

PPF interest is calculated monthly based on the lowest balance in the account between the **5th and the last day of the month**. Therefore, to maximize interest, you should deposit your annual contribution before the 5th of April, or make monthly deposits before the 5th of every month. The interest is credited to the account once a year on March 31st.
